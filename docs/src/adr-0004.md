# ADR 0004: Decompose plan-shaped evidence into measurement and policy

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-09-06
- Extends: ADR 0002 (extract by moving contracts), ADR 0003 (durable identity)

## Context

ADR 0002 named step 3 as decomposing the 23 `*PlanSpec`-shaped
`GeometryProvider` methods into neutral evidence plus policy in
`axioval-rules`. Measuring those 23 methods shows the problem is not
that they take a plan argument. It is what they return.

Of the 23 plan-shaped methods, **17 return a verdict, not a measurement**:
their result type is a `findings: Vec<Resolved*Finding>` list where each
finding carries a `kind` drawn from a rule-specific enum. For example
`ResolvedFreeFloorSpace` returns `FreeFloorSpaceFindingKind` variants such
as `CircleDoesNotFit`, `NarrowCorridor` and `FurnitureBothSidesTooClose`.
The remaining 6 (`ResolvedHorizontalGuardFacts`, `ResolvedSpaceValidation`,
`ResolvedSlabContact`, `ResolvedExternalWallValidation`,
`ResolvedShelfCapacity`, `ResolvedSpaceTopology`) are measurement-shaped.

The consequence is visible in the rule layer. `StairRule` is 112 LOC and
its entire body is:

```rust
let issues = facts.findings.into_iter().map(|finding| {
    Issue::new(self.id(), Severity::Warning, format!("stair: {:?}", finding.kind), finding.elements)
}).collect();
```

It relabels decisions someone else made. The compliance logic lives in
`adapters/ifc/production/accessibility/stair.rs`, which is 1,645 LOC --
a 15:1 ratio of policy in the provider versus policy in the rule.

This is why the sidecar waves could not amortize. Porting a rule to the
engine means porting its private verdict producer with it, and that
producer is written against `IfcModel` and `EntityRef`. There is no
reusable evidence underneath to port onto.

By contrast `axioval-engine` already models this correctly.
`FreeSpaceService` exposes `measure_free_area(&FreeAreaRequest) ->
FreeAreaEvidence`, where the evidence carries an `AreaInterval` and
nothing else. The verdict is computed by capability policy in
`axioval-rules`. The same domain, decomposed the right way round.

## Decision

Step 3 is a **decomposition, not a relocation**. For each plan-shaped
method, the verdict enum is split into two parts:

1. **Measurement** -- the quantity the provider actually computed
   (a clearance distance, an inscribed radius, a slope, a contact area).
   This is source-specific work and stays behind a neutral service trait
   parameterized by geometry and identity only.
2. **Policy** -- the comparison of that quantity against declared
   parameters that produces the finding kind. This is source-neutral and
   moves to `axioval-rules` as capability policy.

A finding kind such as `CircleDoesNotFit` is not evidence. It is the
result of comparing a measured inscribed radius against a declared
minimum. The measurement is portable; the comparison is policy.

Ordering follows the measurement-shaped methods first: those 6 already
return quantities and need only a neutral request/response type, so they
validate the seam before the 17 verdict-shaped ones are split.

## Consequences

- No rule ports before its measurement exists as neutral evidence. This
  is stricter than ADR 0002 implied and is the reason the earlier waves
  stalled at property rules, which were already measurement-shaped.
- `adapters/ifc/production` (11,953 LOC) is not evidence code. It is
  policy wearing an evidence signature, and most of it becomes
  `axioval-rules` capability policy rather than vendor adapter code.
- The gate from ADR 0002 forbids new `*PlanSpec` service arguments, which
  prevents the shape from being recreated. It does not detect a
  verdict-shaped return, so the gate is extended here.

## Enforcement

`scripts/architecture.py` rejects a service trait method whose return
type names a finding, violation or compliance verdict. A service returns
what was measured; a capability decides what it means.
