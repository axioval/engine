# Free space and clearance

Free-space computation is a typed host service. The engine owns validated source-neutral requests and evidence; Axiolid or another trusted geometry provider owns rasterization, CSG, collision tests, search, and spatial indexes.

## Fixed clearance volumes

`ClearanceRequest` combines:

- an object-grounded `MetricFrame` in canonical metres;
- orthonormal right, forward, and up directions;
- a validated box or cylinder;
- a deterministic source-qualified obstacle candidate set selected by the trusted rule capability.

The frame's origin is the centre of the volume's base: a box extends half its width either side along `right`, half its depth either side along `forward`, and its height up from the origin; a cylinder is centred on the origin. Semantic filtering stays outside geometry: the provider evaluates exactly the supplied candidate objects. This represents directional component clearance without leaking IFC placements, meshes, B-reps, or kernel types into the engine.

The result is asymmetric:

- `Obstructed` needs one exact, non-empty obstruction witness;
- `Clear` needs exact and complete obstacle coverage for the request.

Partial geometry therefore cannot produce a false clear result.

The Axiolid adapter measures a box as the exact rectangle along the frame's right and forward axes, swept over its height, and refuses a tilted frame. It bounds a cylinder from both sides with prisms over inscribed and circumscribed 64-gons. An obstacle meeting the inscribed prism obstructs; the volume is clear only when every obstacle misses the circumscribed one. An obstacle between the two (within about 0.12 % of the radius) refuses the request rather than being guessed either way.

An obstacle obstructs only when its solid and the volume share interior. Its height range and plan outline are never tested separately: an L-shaped body with a low foot under the volume and a tall column beside it overlaps the volume in both, yet never enters it. The adapter tests the solid against the prism shrunk by 1 µm on every side, so a body resting on the base, standing against a side or touching the top leaves the volume clear:

- a triangle of the obstacle meets the shrunk prism: the part of the triangle inside the height band is a convex polygon, which meets the prism exactly when it meets the prism's plan polygon, a separating-axis test between two convex polygons. This also catches an obstacle wholly inside the volume;
- otherwise the prism lies wholly inside or wholly outside the obstacle, and the winding number at the volume's centre, far from every triangle, tells which.

Only a closed, consistently and outward wound mesh bounds a solid. An obstacle whose mesh box reaches the shrunk prism must be one, or the request is refused; one whose box misses it is clear whatever its mesh.

## Containment in scopes

`ContainmentRequest` asks whether the same volume's plan footprint lies inside the union of the footprints of source-qualified scopes, such as the spaces a component stands in (merged spaces are several scopes). Only the plan is compared. The scopes are the rule's selection; with none, nothing covers the footprint.

- `Inside` claims that no part of the footprint of positive area lies outside every scope;
- `Outside` claims that some part does.

Both carry exact evidence bound to the request. The Axiolid adapter takes the overlay difference of the footprint's bounds less the scopes' projected triangles, so the part outside is measured directly rather than as a small difference of two large areas: the outer bound left with nothing is `Inside`, the inner bound left with something is `Outside`, and a cylinder whose band straddles a scope boundary is refused. A scope without a body, unmeasured or tessellated is refused. A service that does not implement containment refuses by default, never answering either way.

## Support coverage

`SupportCoverageRequest` asks whether the tops of source-qualified supports hold the same volume's plan footprint: the upward-facing surfaces of their bodies, clipped to an elevation band (`from_metres` to `to_metres`), such as a slab or landing near the floor under a door's clear area. Only the plan and the band are compared. The supports are the rule's selection; with none, nothing holds the footprint, and a band that is not finite or reversed is refused.

- `Supported` claims that no part of the footprint of positive area lies outside the tops in the band;
- `Unsupported` claims that some part does.

Both carry exact evidence bound to the request (`SupportCoverageEvidence`). The Axiolid adapter takes every triangle of a support facing up, clips it to the band and projects it, then measures the overlay difference of the footprint's bounds less those tops, as for containment: the outer bound left with nothing is `Supported`, the inner bound left with something is `Unsupported`, and a cylinder whose band straddles a top's edge is refused. A support without a body, unmeasured or tessellated is refused. `FreeSpaceService::assess_support_coverage` refuses by default, never answering either way, and the handle refuses an answer to another request. `component-clearance` asks it twice: the union of the volume's positions against sure supports within the band every possible base shares (supported), and their common part against every possible support within the band any base reaches (unsupported).

## Component clearance


`axioval:capability.component-clearance` places a fixed box or cylinder beside each selected component in the component's own placement frame (see [Capabilities](./capabilities.md#free-space-around-components)). Which frame axis is the component's front is the rule's statement (`front_axis`), or the front the object-frame service states; it is never inferred from the placement axes, the shape or the type, and IFC states none. A door's swing side is stated, though: `front_axis` `swing` takes the direction its hinged leaves open towards from its leaves, with world up, so a door placed upside down is measured upright. With `front_axis` `against-wall` the front is derived from the walls beside the sides of the footprint's least-area rectangle: the side surely nearest a wall is the back, a tie is not decided, and the derived front is inexact evidence naming the wall. The volume starts at the component's outermost point on the stated side (or its midline, with `depth_from` `midline`), measured as directional extents, so it follows the body rather than the placement origin. A width, depth or height sized from the component is measured the same way, as an interval, and the volume is kept as the intervals its edges and its base and top are known to: its union is bounded by the outermost ends of those intervals, its common part by the innermost.

Every measured position is an interval, so the volume's position is too. The capability asks two questions: whether the union of every position the volume could take is clear (then it is clear wherever it is), and whether the part every position shares is obstructed (then it is obstructed wherever it is). Along a coordinate axis the intervals are points and one request answers both. Anything between is not evaluated.

A floating volume, one that may slide sideways or away from the component until it fits, is a placement search below instead: the scope is the first space `space_path` reaches, merged with the others; the domain is a frame-offset domain anchored on the component, with the side's plan axes, the slide interval across (`slide_from`, `slide_to`), the slide interval away from it (`depth_slide_from`, `depth_slide_to`) and the base on the scope's floor; the box is fixed to the anchor's axes; the band runs from the base up to the top above the floor. The union and common part become the volume grown and shrunk by the position interval. A found witness is a volume free wherever the component is (along an axis it does not slide on, the grown volume is also searched 1 mm larger each way within 1 mm of the line, so the search is not confined to a line, which has no area); a proof of no placement for the shrunk volume is a volume obstructed at every offset. A maximum size is asked as the volume one tolerance larger in each dimension, fixed or floating, with the answers swapped: free is the finding.

Accessible routes use the same search for passing spaces: frame-offset domains anchored on each segment of the walked route, one tile of the route at a time (see [Capabilities](./capabilities.md#passing-spaces)).

## Placement search

`PlacementRequest` asks whether a box or cylinder can fit in a source-qualified search scope. Its `PlacementShape` states which rotations count, because "fits" depends on it: a box that fits only diagonally has no placement along a fixed frame but has one at some angle.

- A box carries a `PlacementOrientation`. `Fixed(frame)` binds the box width to the frame's right axis and its depth to the forward axis; only the axes are binding, not the frame origin. The frame comes from the rule's context, such as a door leaf, a fixture or a declared room axis, and never defaults silently to the model's world axes. `Any` quantifies over every rotation about the vertical axis.
- A cylinder is rotation-invariant and carries no orientation.

A `Found` witness for a fixed orientation must use exactly the fixed axes; any other frame is rejected as `PlacementOrientationMismatch`. Evidence for a fixed orientation answers only that orientation: "no 1.80 × 1.80 m area aligned with the corridor" does not mean "no area at any angle".

A `PlacementDomain` makes the admissible candidate set explicit:

- `Unconstrained` preserves generic searches without implying support;
- `Supported` requires the whole candidate base on a source-qualified support object within a maximum gap;
- `FrameOffsets` limits right/forward/up translation in an anchor frame;
- `SupportedFrameOffsets` requires both support and anchor-relative bounds.

Frame-offset witnesses keep the anchor's axes, so a box searched in a frame-offset domain must be `Fixed` to those axes. Any other orientation is refused as `OrientationDomainConflict` when the request is built. The anchor may be grounded on another object than the scope, such as the door or fixture whose frame the object-frame service states, with the front axis the rule's statement; the witness is still grounded on the scope. `FrameOffsetPlacement::contains_frame` is the test every witness must pass, and backends may filter with it.

Two request options shape the search:

- `with_band(ElevationBand)` states the band, from and to metres above the scope's floor, in which obstacles count. Without it the band runs from the floor up by the shape's height (`effective_band`). A band must start at or above the floor and end above its start.
- `with_merged_scopes` searches the union of the scope and further scopes, such as the spaces of one group. A merged scope may be neither the scope nor an obstacle (`MergedScopeConflict`).
- `with_swept_doors` counts the floor the given doors' leaves sweep as obstacles (see [Door swings as obstacles](#door-swings-as-obstacles)).
- `with_entrance_reach(EntranceReach)` requires the shape to be reached from entrances by a path of a width: it must meet a piece of the free area eroded by half the path's width that comes within half the width plus a tolerance of an entrance, as a [circulation map](#circulation-maps) proves a contact. The path is free in the same band, clear of the same obstacles and swept doors; the entrances are walked through, so the request drops them from the obstacles and their swings from the swept doors. Without entrances nothing is reached.

- `Found` carries one exact, scope-grounded placement frame. Supported domains additionally require exact evidence that the whole candidate base is supported by the requested source-qualified object at that exact frame and within the requested gap.
- `NoPlacement` requires complete exact search evidence.

A bounded or partial search cannot claim that no placement exists.

### The Axiolid placement search

The Axiolid adapter decides placement in the scope's configuration space: the set of centres at which the shape lies in the scope footprint and meets no obstacle in its height band. That set is the scope eroded by the shape, minus every obstacle footprint dilated by the reflected shape. A point in it is a witness; an empty set proves that no placement exists. When the obstacle footprints of a polygon shape hold more than 500 corners (a floor's walls united into one region with a hole per room), the scope less the obstacles is eroded by the shape instead (#359): the same centres and those at which the shape only touches an obstacle, so a witness is still verified by overlap and an absence proven there holds too. Summing a floor's walls with the shape built one arrangement that outgrew 12 GB on a large floor of one model, and eroding is faster there.

The height band runs from the scope's floor (the bottom of its body) up by the shape's height, or over the request's elevation band, and is open at both ends: a body resting on the floor under the shape or starting exactly at its top does not block it. As in clearance, an obstacle counts only by the part of its solid inside the band, never by its height range and plan outline taken apart: its band footprint is its boundary clipped to the band, plus, for a body reaching down to the floor, its section just above the floor. So an L-shaped body with a column along a wall and an arm overhead blocks only the column's strip. Clipping planar triangles to horizontal planes is exact. The clipped faces reach the plan overlay as triangles, so the shadow of a face standing on edge, whose corners lie on one line only up to rounding, is never taken for a self-intersecting ring; corners of neighbouring faces that come out a few units in the last place apart are snapped together, by at most a nanometre, far below the margin every witness keeps. A footprint the overlay still refuses refuses the search with the obstacle's name. A body reaching down to the floor must be closed and consistently wound, or the search refuses, since its section is the non-zero winding of its boundary. Closure is read from the positions, not the indices, piece by piece (#309): the body is split into the pieces its surface connects, corners with equal coordinates taken as one; each piece reaching down to the floor must use every edge as often one way as the other, zero-area triangles included (a T-junction closed by a sliver bounds a solid, as in clash, #221), and only those pieces give the section. A piece that stays off the floor counts by its surface, as a whole body off the floor does, so a closed fitting on the floor beside an open sheet higher up is measured; an open piece on the floor still refuses. The sure core of a tessellated obstacle (below) needs the same of the pieces standing across its column. An obstacle without a measured body refuses too. At georeferenced coordinates (about 6e6 m, where a few units in the last place already exceed a nanometre) the snapping starts at those units and stays a hundredth of the witness margin at most (#304).

An obstacle whose plan box, grown by its chord deviation, is apart from the scope's (grown by the scope's largest deviation) is left out before its band footprint is built: a shape inside the scope cannot meet it, since every centre the eroded scope keeps lies outside its dilation. Every object of a model is an obstacle candidate of every space, so without this a space's search built and dilated the band footprints of every tessellated body on its floor, however far away. An unmeasured obstacle refuses the search, unless the host bounds it (`with_unmeasured_bound`, such as a part whose whole's opening cannot be subtracted, a clipped body by its first operand, or a body refused for a face by its authored vertices; see [the CLI](./cli.md)) apart from the scope the same way, or entirely below or above the band; a bound of several boxes (a whole by its parts, a body by its items) is apart when each of its boxes is (a slab whose top is the floor: the band is open at both ends).

A tessellated scope or obstacle is bracketed by its chord deviation `d` (#302), never refused for being near. A witness avoids everything the obstacle may occupy: its band footprint in the band widened by `d` at both ends, grown by `d` (`Region::dilate_outer`). A proof of absence holds against what it surely occupies: the plan points where a vertical column of height `2d`, a deviation above the band's bottom, lies inside its mesh, eroded by `d` (`Region::erode_inner`); a point deeper than `d` inside the mesh lies inside the true body. A tessellated scope is eroded by its deviation for a witness and grown by it for a proof, and a floor its deviation leaves uncertain widens the band for witnesses and narrows it for proofs. A body whose vertical bounds keep it out of the band occupies nothing either way: a slab whose top meets the floor rises into the room by its deviation unless its extent is certified, so without one every fit above it is refused. Two certificates narrow it, combined where both are given: the height its construction states (`AxiolidGeometry::with_vertical_extent_bounds`, #393), which the CLI reads from the graph whatever the outline (an extrusion with horizontal caps spans them exactly, a difference lies within its subject's; see [the CLI](./cli.md)), and the extent of its exact boundary (`with_extent_bounds`: the subject's closed form outside, its vertices inside). The band is open at both ends, so a body whose certified top is at or below the floor (a curved slab under the room, a round column or wall of the storey below) or whose certified bottom is at or above the band's top is not in it; a body truly crossing into it (a duct or stair flight rising through the floor) is bracketed within its own band footprint as above, never across the floor. A curved space's own floor is certified the same way, so its deviation no longer widens the band. The bracketing morphology runs in a plan frame at the scope's corner, where the overlay keeps its slivers; moving there is exact. The verdict then holds for every body within the deviation, so its evidence stays exact, and its locator names what was bracketed (`:within-chord-deviation=<object>=<d>m,…`). What the bracket leaves undecided (a fit within `d` of the shape's size) is refused as inexact placement evidence, never decided; a deviation that bounds nothing refuses as before. The bracket is the clearance rule (#393): a witness stands when its clearance to every tessellated body exceeds that body's deviation (measured as a box of the deviation, which holds its ball), so a large room with curved furniture holds a shape with room to spare; a body touching the band whose height nothing certifies (a horizontal pipe whose round top meets the floor, read without its exact boundary) has no clearance and still refuses.

An obstacle that cannot be measured but has a certified enclosing bound is a possible obstacle within that bound (#401), never a reason to leave the space undecided: a witness that avoids a superset of the body avoids the body, and an absence proven without it holds with it, as for an obstacle the selection cannot decide. Two bounds are certified. An unmeasured body the host encloses in a closed solid (`AxiolidGeometry::with_unmeasured_enclosure`, the solid's mesh with its chord deviation and certified elevations) takes that solid's band footprint, in the band widened by its deviation and grown by it, and nothing surely; the CLI encloses a body whose difference the mesh compiler refuses (a curved wall less openings the exact boolean cannot cut) in its subject, the first one along its differences it can measure (`difference_subjects`: the gross body before the openings, then an authored difference's subject), and a part a whole's opening cannot be subtracted from in its uncut body (see [the CLI](./cli.md)). An open mesh whose pieces reaching into the band have no inside (a sanitary terminal authored as an open shell standing on the floor), whose band footprint is therefore refused, may occupy the hull of its vertices: every point of a triangle lies in the hull of its corners, and the faces the surface lacks span its vertices too. The hull is computed so that a point is dropped only where its turn is certainly not to the left (the orientation determinant's static error bound), so every vertex is held, and it is grown by a square of half side the chord deviation plus the coordinates' rounding, at least 1 µm, before it joins the obstacles (a polygon, so the scene gains no arcs); where the overlay refuses the hull's ring, the vertices' box stands for it. The closed pieces of such a body still surely occupy what they do, so an absence they prove stands. A body the band leaves out by its vertical bounds is left out as before, and an obstacle with no certified bound still refuses the search. The locator of a placement, a free floor or a circulation map decided around bounds names them (`:bounded-not-measured=<object>,…`); free floor counts each as possibly taking the floor its bound shares with the room, which a failed free-floor finding reports as "perhaps also taken by"; and a search the bounds leave undecided is unavailable with the reason "the obstacles bounded rather than measured (<object>, …) leave the placement undecided".

A circle among more obstacle corners than 500 erodes the scope less the obstacles by the disc rather than dilating the obstacles by exact arcs, as the rectangle has since #359 (#393); smaller scenes are searched as before. A placement scene is a derivation of the check's store (ADR 0006, #408): it is built once per check from everything it is built from (`SceneKey`: the service's geometry, scope, merged scopes, obstacles, swept doors, the band's limits and the tolerance by their bits, the precision class and a version), whatever rule, search or thread asks for it first, and read by every other: a space is searched several times over one scene (undecided obstacles first and again without them, with a path and without, the bracket of the largest shape, #391), and circle searches, rectangle searches, largest-disc estimates and circulation maps over the same space, obstacles and band share it across rules. A second thread asking for a scene being built waits for it (single flight). The scene keeps what its searches took (clear parts, their largest discs, simplified and coarse regions, the regions found for a rectangle), merged back after each search; every search is charged them as if it had built them (#434, #441), so a scene kept, shared or built again answers the same. The store keeps the adapter's scenes and indexed bodies up to a quarter of the check's memory budget each (1 GiB without one), held against the budget, and past it releases the scenes read least recently; releasing a scene when the plan's last rule reading it has run is #440. A scene's precision class is Exact for every request (#438): a Fast scene, whose bracketing morphology runs in the overlay's fast mode within its budget and which carries the greatest excess that reported, is another entry, never read for an Exact one, and an Exact scene keeps no region stating an excess (ADR 0006, [Precision classes](./adr-0006.md#precision-classes)).

`FreeSpaceService::estimate_largest_disc` (#400) answers where the largest disc of a request's free floor probably lies, as a `LargestDiscEstimate`: a radius at which the backend expects `find_placement` to place a disc and one at which it expects it to prove one absent. It is a hint, never evidence: the free-floor capabilities search those sizes first when they bracket the largest shape, and state a size only from the searches. The default refuses. The Axiolid service estimates both from the scene's clear parts (the scope less the obstacles for a witness, the room less the sure ones for an absence), kept with the scene: the pole of inaccessibility of each, refined over at most 4096 cells (`placement::inscribed_disc`), shrunk and grown by the 64-gons that verify a witness and prove an absence. The circle search uses the same estimate: where the witnesses' clear part holds no disc of the radius, it asks the absence first and the witness only where the absence is not proven, which gives the answer asking the witness first does (a witness's region lies inside the absence's) at one disc morphology instead of two. A disc that fits at the estimated pole of the witnesses' clear part is verified there directly, with no morphology at all. Each search is asked first over its regions simplified on the side it may use them (corners within 0.1 mm of the chord between their neighbours cut off where a witness is sought, filled where an absence is proven, each checked to lie on its side, and none where it would keep more than three quarters of the corners, #435): overlay output along tessellated or flush bodies is jagged with sub-millimetre edges, and a disc morphology's cost grows faster than its edges. Only where the simplified regions leave the circle undecided is it searched again over the regions as built. The disc morphology takes the overlay's fast mode with a 1 mm budget (#414, axiolid-overlay 0.3.18): a notch whose filling by a chord adds at most that much is filled, and each result stays on the side the search asks of it, inside the exact erosion for a witness and around it for an absence, so a witness and an absence hold as over the exact morphology, and a circle within the excess of fitting is undecided. Where no notch lies within the budget the result is the exact one bit for bit. A region of more than 100,000 corners is never eroded or dilated by a disc: the search is refused, naming the count (#394). The bound was 1,000 corners until axiolid-overlay 0.3.16 removed the morphology's quadratic arrangement (#402), 5,000 until the fast mode (#414), 20,000 until the search stopped simplifying regions it could hardly simplify (#435), and 50,000 until axiolid-overlay 0.3.19 checked each ring against itself once, in time about its corners, rather than with every difference or witness check, in time growing with their square (#445): around a column toothed 0.5 mm deep, an erosion by a 0.4 m disc took 14 to 36 s and 7.5 GB at 10,000 corners exactly and takes 0.04 s fast, 0.08 s at 20,000 and 0.2 s at 50,000, and a whole search, both verdicts, took 21 s and 250 MB at 50,000 corners, 12 s of it simplifying the free floor by 16 corners, then 4.5 s and 130 MB, and 18 s at 100,000, and takes 0.55 s at 50,000, 1.1 s and 230 MB at 100,000 and 2.3 s and 440 MB at 200,000. Where the room is the scope and every obstacle sure, both sides' free floor is one difference, and a witness is checked against the obstacles only where one lies within a millimetre of its box. A disc wider than the region it would erode, in the narrowest direction of the region's convex hull, is never eroded either (#445): both erosions are empty, so the step is charged as before and its result taken empty. The bound stays as a safety net: past some 50,000 corners the overlay's fast erosion of a floor toothed along a wall, by a disc whose radius exceeds a third of its width, builds from nearly every tooth, in time and memory the search's budget does not count (up to 7.7 s and 0.9 GB at 100,000 corners, 15 s and 4.2 GB at 150,000 to 200,000), and teeth far deeper than they are apart fill no notch within the budget.

A rectangle's proofs are asked first over coarse regions (#427). A piece of curved furniture tessellated into a few hundred edges of some millimetres costs the overlay more than half a second to sum with a 1.8 m rectangle, since every edge's hull overlaps every other's, and an angle search sums it once per interval. So each scene keeps its sure obstacles simplified from inside (corners within 1 mm of the chord between their neighbours cut off, the result checked to lie inside), used only where that leaves at most three quarters of the corners. An interval's absence is asked first with them: fewer obstacles leave more centres, so where those are none the exact ones are none too, and only where centres are left is it asked again over the regions as built. The proofs over every angle at once take the same way: the room less the coarse sure obstacles for `Nowhere`, the scope less the obstacles simplified from inside for no witness at any angle, and the room less the sure obstacles simplified from around for a fit somewhere. A proof found this way is one the regions as built give, so verdicts are unchanged; witnesses are never sought over coarse regions, so every found centre is the one it was. The scene also keeps each rectangle's configuration space by the rectangle's corners, at most 250,000 corners in all: a space is searched over one scene with a path and without, and every search starts at the same angles, so the second takes the first's regions as they were. On a private four-model house of 17 spaces, a 1.8 × 1.8 m rectangle at any orientation reached by a 0.9 m path took 24.1 s on one thread before and 15.2 s after, 443 ms per search before and 280 ms after; its two heaviest spaces, each furnished with such a piece, went from 6.6 s to 2.3 s for the first search and from 1.3 s to 0.57 s for the second. What is left there is the first interval's witness over the regions as built and mapping the path; rectangles without a path, whose rooms hold no such piece, take as long as before.

Rectangle searches ask the same questions again, and an angle search learns as it goes (#427). A space that no rectangle of the size asked fits is searched up to ten times more: the free-floor rules bracket the largest rectangle of the declared proportions (#391), some sizes twice. On m13 (157 spaces) among every physical element, that bracket was 95% of the 1.8 × 1.5 m search, and 83% went to searches that ran out of their 512 intervals: a rectangle that neither fits nor is excluded near some angle, towards which the depth-first descent narrows its intervals until floating point halves them no more and the last ones repeat. Nearly all of each search is the overlay's Minkowski sum of the obstacles with a rectangle, once per interval for the coarse absence, once for the exact one and once for the witness.

- A scene keeps every angle search asked of it, by the rectangle's sizes, the reached pieces and the work it started from, with its verdict and the work it ended at, and takes a search asked again as it ended (`Scene::searched`, at most 256): its steps and verdict are a function of those alone.
- The coarse proofs' centres are kept by the rectangle's corners as the configuration spaces are, so an interval asked again takes them.
- After its first interval, without a reach, a search learns within itself (`Learnt`, never kept past it). It keeps up to 16 centres at which an absence's shrunk rectangle was seen to stand with 0.01 mm to spare inside the room and clear of the sure obstacles, tested in floating point edge by edge (`Scene::stands`): where the next interval's shrunk rectangle stands at one, its absence cannot be proven, and none of its steps is taken. Where the coarse proof leaves centres, one of them standing among the obstacles as built shows the same, and so do centres left among the sure obstacles simplified from around (`Scene::stands_somewhere`), which hold them: more obstacles, fewer centres. Only where none decides is the absence asked over the regions as built.
- Where an interval's witness region is empty, its halves first ask the witness region of their shrunk rectangle, which every rotation within them holds: where that is empty too, no rotation in the interval has a witness, and none is sought there or below. After a miss the next try waits for an interval eight times narrower.

Each of these decides only what the step it spares would have: a centre standing with room to spare lies in the centres that step computes (the margin is far above the overlay's snapping), and a region it skips is empty exactly where that step finds it so. Witnesses are sought, verified and reported over the regions as built as before, so every verdict and found centre is the one it was, and reports are byte-identical on the public parity models and the private houses measured. A step a search spares is neither built nor charged to its budget, which bounds what is built; a search refused by its budget before may decide now, and none on the models measured was. On m13 among every physical element the check took 1712 s and takes 489 s on four threads (4220 s and 1191 s on one), on m07 100.1 s and 24.8 s on one ([Performance](./performance.md#rectangle-searches-that-learn-as-they-go)).

Every search is bounded in memory and time (#434). A Minkowski sum or erosion hands the overlay one arrangement: the region's own rings and, for each of its edges, the hull of the shape moved to both ends. Along a tessellated body of short edges every hull meets every other within the shape's reach, so the arrangement grows with the square of the corners nearby: a 20 m hall crowded with round tables of 128 facets, eroded by a 1.8 m rectangle, took 20 s and 9.7 GB for one angle, and an angle search asks for one per interval. Before each step is built, the search counts the pairs of edges whose boxes meet in what the step hands over, exactly and in `O(n log n)`, the hulls before the overlay trims them (`planar::budget::minkowski_cost`), and refuses the step past its kind's budget:

| Step | Budget, pairs | Measured at the budget |
|---|---|---|
| Minkowski sum or erosion by a rectangle or an octagon | 2,000,000 | about 4 s and 2 GB |
| Disc morphology by the overlay's 64-gon (the path's reach, a circulation map's pieces) | 16,000,000 | about 4 s and 0.9 GB: the overlay trims a disc's hulls along dense outlines to about a tenth of the count |
| Overlay of two regions (differences and intersections between the steps) | 4,000,000 | as for every region boolean (#419, #433): the kernel has no budgeted region boolean, so the pairs bound its memory too |

A search also spends at most what 100 steps at their budgets would together: each step spends its pairs' share of its kind's budget, so a search whose every step passes still cannot run for hours. A region the scene keeps (a clear part, a coarse region, a rectangle's configuration space) is charged as it was when it was built, once per search, so what a search may spend depends on its request alone and never on the searches before it. Past either budget the space is not evaluated, its reason naming the step, its edges and pairs and the budget, and the check goes on with every other space and rule. On the public parity models and two private houses of 17 and 157 spaces, the heaviest search spent 42 of the 100 steps (83 s) and its largest step about half its budget. The count bounds what the arrangement is handed from above; an exact count of the trimmed hulls would need the overlay to state it.

| Shape | Witness (`Found`) | Proof of absence (`NoPlacement`) |
|---|---|---|
| Box, `Fixed` | a centre in the exact configuration space, re-verified by direct overlap | the configuration space is empty even for the box shrunk by 1 µm per side |
| Box, `Any` | a fixed-orientation witness at the middle of an angle interval | every interval is empty for the middle box shrunk by the half-diagonal times the half-interval |
| Cylinder | a centre in the configuration space built from inner disc approximations, which lie inside the exact one | the configuration space built from outer disc approximations, which contain the exact one, is empty for a radius 1 µm smaller |

Anything in between refuses: a fit by contact, a circle whose fit lies inside the disc approximation band (about 0.12 % of the radius), or an angle search whose budget (512 fixed-orientation checks over half a turn, a quarter turn for a square) runs out before every interval is decided. The shrink margins keep rounding from turning a fit by contact into a false proof of absence.

The search runs on the scope's own floor: `Unconstrained` and `Supported` by the scope itself, each also with frame offsets. Other supports are refused until their support rules are written down. With merged scopes the footprint searched is the union of all of them; they must share the scope's floor (within 1 nm), every one is bracketed by its own deviation like the scope, and only an unsupported domain is answered, since no single support holds a base spanning several of them. A `Fixed` frame must be upright. The witness frame stands at the floor elevation and uses the requested axes for `Fixed`, the anchor's for a cylinder in a frame-offset domain, or the found angle for `Any`. Evidence cites the scope's own source; its locator names the merged scopes.

A frame-offset domain is searched exactly for fixed orientations: the configuration space is intersected with the box of centres the right and forward offsets allow. The anchor must be exactly upright, and the scope's floor must lie within the up offsets, or the search refuses rather than proving the domain empty. A witness comes from the offset box as computed and must also pass `contains_frame`; a proof of absence needs the configuration space of the shrunk shape to miss even the box grown by 1 µm, so rounding the box's corners cannot hide a centre on its edge. A box pinned to one offset has no area: the configuration space's candidates are moved onto it and re-verified, and its absence is proven with the grown box. For a cylinder the same intersection applies to the inner and outer configuration spaces.

With an entrance reach, the search first maps the path as a circulation map does: the pieces of the free area eroded by half the path's width from inside that meet an entrance's footprint grown from inside (surely reached), and those eroded from outside, for half the width less 1 µm, that meet it grown from outside (possibly reached). A witness must also lie where its shape meets a surely reached piece (the configuration space intersected with the pieces' Minkowski sum with the shape, or their inner disc dilation for a circle) and is re-verified by overlap: the rectangle, or the circle's inscribed 64-gon, must share area with them. A proof of absence needs the shrunk shape's configuration space to miss the possibly reached pieces' sum with the shape grown by 1 µm (their outer dilation by the radius and 1 µm for a circle); for `Any`, the middle rectangle grown by the interval's shrink covers every rotation in it. The locator names the entrances and the path width.

Swept door sectors join the obstacles as their circumscribed polygons (32 chords per quarter turn) for every witness, and as their inscribed polygons for every proof of absence; with no swept door both are the same obstacles.

Witness candidates are the free region's centroids and the middle of every interval a line halfway between two consecutive vertex heights cuts from it, so a free region with a hole (a room around a column) is never missed because its centroids fall into the hole.

A free corridor width (a region eroded by half the width that still connects two sides) is not searched here: the sides are not part of a placement request. Within one space, a [circulation map](#circulation-maps) answers it between entrances and components; across spaces, corridor widths use metric routing.

Connected corridor requirements use [metric routing](./metric-routing.md) with an appropriate mobility profile rather than inventing a second path contract.

## Free floor

`FreeSpaceService::measure_free_floor` answers a `PlacementRequest` with `FreeFloorEvidence`: what the request's obstacles and swept doors leave of its scope's floor (with merged scopes, theirs too) within the request's band, the shape's height where none is stated. The shape, domain and entrance reach do not change the floor. It is a measurement, never a verdict:

- `area`, an `AreaInterval`: from below the scope as a witness sees it less everything that may take floor (obstacles grown by their chord deviation, swept sectors' circumscribed polygons), from above the scope as an absence is proven in less what surely does. With exact bodies and no swing the two ends agree up to rounding.
- `obstacles`: the request's obstacles and swept doors whose footprint in the band surely shares area with the floor; `possible`: those that may. Both are sorted and disjoint, and name only the request's obstacles and swept doors (`FreeFloorEvidence::try_new` refuses others). A body flush with the floor's edge takes none.

The default refuses. The Axiolid service builds the placement scene once, keeping each obstacle's footprints apart as it does (`Taken`): an obstacle surely takes floor where what it surely occupies shares area with the witnesses' scope, possibly where what it may occupy shares more than 1 mm² with the room (a wall flush with a scope bracketed around its slivers shares some 1e-8 m², no floor anyone stands on). Its evidence is exact, `axiolid:free-floor:<scope>` with the merged scopes and the bracketed bodies named as a placement's are. The free-floor capabilities read it for a proven absence ([Built-ins](./capabilities.md#built-ins)).

## Circulation maps

`FreeSpaceService::map_circulation` answers a `CirculationRequest`: a space, its entrances, its components, the obstacles, a path width, the height the path must be free to and a tolerance. Entrances are never obstacles and the space is none of the three; the constructor removes them. A service that does not map circulation refuses by default. The `CirculationMap` is a measurement:

- **Pieces**: the connected parts of a region inside the space's free area (its floor less what the obstacles occupy in the band from the floor up by the height) eroded by half the width. Every point of a piece is the centre of a free disc as wide as the path, so a path runs between any two points of one piece.
- **Possible pieces**: the connected parts of a region containing that erosion. A path between two different possible pieces does not exist.
- **Contacts**: for each entrance and component (the request's subjects, sorted), the pieces proven to come within half the width plus the tolerance of its plan footprint, each with the skeleton node nearest to it, and the possible pieces that may.
- **Skeleton**: nodes (`CirculationNode`: a point on the floor, a kind by its number of neighbours, `End`, `Path`, `Junction` or `Isolated`, its piece, and bounds on its distance to the free area's boundary, the half width there) and edges within one piece. Node positions are approximate: that a node lies in its piece and its half width are proven, how close it is to the true medial axis is not. The map states the boundary sample spacing it was built with.
- **Unmapped pieces**: pieces whose skeleton could not be built, with why; their contacts still stand, but where their paths end is unknown.

Two request options shape the map: `with_merged_scopes` maps the union of the space and further spaces standing on its floor (merged spaces are never obstacles, and may be neither an entrance nor a component: `MergedScopeConflict`), and `with_band_from` starts the band obstacles count in above the floor, below the height (`InvalidElevationBand` otherwise). The evidence locator names the merged spaces.

`CirculationMap::try_new` checks the map's shape: nodes in pieces, edges within one piece, kinds matching the neighbours, one contact per subject naming only pieces and nodes that are there, and exact evidence. The handle rejects a map of another request.

The Axiolid adapter builds the free area as the placement search's scene does, tessellated bodies bracketed as there: the pieces from the scope's sure part, the possible pieces from its possible part. The pieces are the polygons of the scope eroded by `Region::erode_inner` less the obstacles grown by `dilate_outer`; the possible pieces the polygons of `erode_outer` less `dilate_inner`, for half the width less 1 µm, so a gap exactly as wide as the path keeps a sliver and is neither proven nor ruled out. A contact is proven when a piece meets the subject's footprint grown by `dilate_inner`, possible when a possible piece meets it grown by `dilate_outer`. The skeleton is `axiolid_route::skeleton` of each piece with pruning factor 1.5 and a boundary spacing of a tenth of the width (at most 4000 samples per piece); a disconnected skeleton is built once more with the spacing down to half the narrowest width of the erosion. Half widths are distances to the free area's boundary widened by the overlay's grid snapping. A subject without a body, unmeasured or tessellated refuses the map.

A piece whose skeleton the kernel refuses is unmapped, never guessed.

Swept door sectors (`with_swept_doors`) are obstacles over the whole band: the pieces keep clear of their circumscribed polygons, the possible pieces only of their inscribed ones, and a node's half width runs from its distance to the free area less the former to its distance to the free area less the latter. An entrance's own swing is dropped from the request.

## Door swings as obstacles

A rule may count the floor a door's leaves sweep as an obstacle. It reads the door's leaves (`ObjectFrameService::leaves`) and sends each door as a `SweptDoor`: the door and the horizontal `SwingSector`s of its hinged leaves (`SweptDoor::of`; a sliding or fixed door sweeps nothing and is not sent). Placement (`PlacementRequest::with_swept_doors`), circulation (`CirculationRequest::with_swept_doors`) and walkability (`WalkabilityRequest::with_swept_doors`) requests carry them sorted by door; one door given twice with different sectors is refused (`ConflictingSweptDoors`). Swings are never measured from a door's body.

A swept sector is an obstacle in plan over the whole band of a floor it stands on: its hinge at most `SWEPT_FLOOR_REACH_METRES` (0.5 m) below the floor and below the band's top (`SweptDoor::stands_on`), so a door on the storey above or below never counts. Its footprint is not a polygon, so a backend brackets it with `SwingSector::plan_bounds` and uses each side where it is sound:

- a witness (a placement, a piece of a circulation map, a definite walkability passage) keeps clear of the **circumscribed** polygon, which contains the sector;
- a proof of absence (no placement, a possible piece, a separation between surfaces or pieces) holds against the **inscribed** polygon, which the sector contains.

An entrance is walked through. A circulation request drops an entrance's own swing, as it drops the entrance from the obstacles; walkability never counts a portal's swing against a passage through that portal, only against passages past it.

## Free-area bounds

`FreeAreaRequest` measures accessible area for a scope and mobility profile. `AreaInterval` stores conservative square-metre bounds. Minimum-area comparisons are three-valued:

- the lower bound meets the minimum: satisfied;
- the upper bound is below the minimum: violated;
- bounds straddle the minimum: indeterminate.

## Runtime boundary

`FreeSpaceServiceHandle` is registered in `ServiceRegistry`. It validates that every response is bound to the exact request. Missing geometry, unsupported composition, resource limits, and incomplete evidence return `FreeSpaceError`; none become clear, no-placement, or passing results.

The contract contains no IFC, STEP, ICDD, Axiolid, OpenCascade, CGAL, or vendor-specific type.
